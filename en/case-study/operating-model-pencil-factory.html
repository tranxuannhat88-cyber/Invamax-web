Operating Model Transformation at a Pencil Factory

From Mini Factory management to specialized process-based management

At a pencil manufacturing facility, the operating model was restructured from Mini Factory (MF) management to specialized process-based management, simplifying managerial roles, boosting resource balancing agility, and optimizing 7 personnel.

Pencil Manufacturing Case Study

The problem lies not in the operation itself, but in how it is organized

Prior to improvement, the factory had 6 Mini Factories (MFs). Each MF leader was responsible for all 4 processes within their Mini Factory.

This siloed structure created critical operational bottlenecks:

  • Overall MF throughput was constrained by the lowest-performing internal process.
  • Cross-process labor and tooling were rigid and difficult to dynamically balance.
  • Production scheduling was sub-optimized with high variance across units.
  • Each MF leader was stretched thin across fundamentally different technical domains.
  • Operating 6 Mini Factories required 6 full-time MF leaders.

Why is the new model more effective?

Specialized Leadership

Leaders specialize in one distinct process rather than juggling disparate operational activities.

Agile Resource Balancing

Resources can be easily balanced across lines based on actual real-time demand.

Transparent Capacity Planning

Capacity is evaluated across the entire process rather than fragmented across isolated units.

Lean Operating Model

Reduces redundant management layers and narrows excessive control spans for leaner execution.
